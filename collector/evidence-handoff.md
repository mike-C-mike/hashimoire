# Digital Evidence — Packaging & Handoff

**Use case:** preservation | chain of custody | handoff  
**Environment:** mobile | computer | removable media  
**Risk:** caution

> Follow evidence-room packaging rules, handling hazards, agency-specific procedures, and examiner instructions. This page is a general public checklist, not a substitute for a formal chain-of-custody record.

## Before packaging

- [ ] Confirm authority and link each item to the incident/evidence identifier.
- [ ] Document who found it, when and where, and initial location/condition.
- [ ] Photograph device, screen state if visible, accessories and connections before changes.
- [ ] Record make/model, serial number or visible identifiers, and any damage.
- [ ] Record the device's initial **on/off and locked/unlocked** state.
- [ ] Record **every** action: touch, disconnection, radio isolation, power supply, screen change, packaging decision.
- [ ] Ask the examiner about powered-on devices or encryption before deciding packaging, transport, or powering down.

## During transfer

- [ ] Separate and label distinct evidence items and related accessories, preserving their association.
- [ ] Protect loose removable media and fragile connectors with suitable packaging.
- [ ] Do not seal operating devices in a way that causes overheating or presents a battery hazard.
- [ ] Document isolation equipment or power support used during transport.
- [ ] Record date/time (with timezone), collector, receiver, condition, and location at each custody transfer.

## Examiner handoff note

The receiving examiner should be able to answer:

**What is it?** Item identifiers, type, accessories, owner/assigned user if lawfully established.

**How was it found?** Location, original screen/lock/power state, visible warnings, connected devices.

**What happened afterward?** Interaction log, radio/network isolation, charging, restarts, damage, packaging, deviations.

**What needs attention now?** Powered-on device, unlocked session, remote wipe concerns, encryption, fragile or hazardous condition.
