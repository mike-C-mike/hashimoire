# Interim Scene Triage & Photography — When CSU Is Unavailable or Delayed

**Audience:** sworn officers | investigators | authorized first responders  
**Use case:** temporary scene preservation | essential photography | evidence location documentation | CSU handoff  
**Risk:** caution  
**Guidance reviewed:** 2026-10-26

> **WHO THIS GUIDE IS FOR — READ FIRST:** This is **not a replacement for a Crime Scene Unit (CSU), crime scene technician, or a full forensic scene examination**. Use it only when (1) your agency **does not have a CSU or a qualified scene responder**, or (2) an **unanticipated delay or urgent threat of evidence loss** requires an authorized officer to document the scene **before CSU can arrive**. **Secure and preserve the scene first**; request CSU/specialist assistance whenever available. Follow your department's scene policy, lawful authority, training, and incident command. A complex, hazardous, fatal, or high-consequence scene generally requires specialist direction rather than improvised processing.

## Decision: should you begin photographing?

| Situation | Appropriate first move |
| --- | --- |
| **CSU is available and the scene is stable** | **Secure it, control access, record initial observations, and wait for CSU.** Do not create unnecessary traffic through the scene just to obtain photos. |
| **CSU delayed and conditions may change** (weather, imminent building work, emergency movement, power failure) | Notify the supervisor/CSU and document the reason, authorization, time and **limited photographs** needed to preserve what may disappear. |
| **No CSU exists in the agency** | An authorized, trained officer may perform a **systematic preliminary photographic record** using the outside-to-inside sequence below, within policy and capability. |
| **Unsafe scene, rescue underway, hazardous materials, active violence** | Life safety and scene stabilization come first. Use specialist responders; never cross an unsafe perimeter to obtain a better image. |
| **Specialized evidence** (complex blood pattern, latent prints, controlled substances, explosives, fatalities, fragile trace) | Photograph from a safe position if authorized; **don't handle, reposition, mark, sample or process** evidence without the necessary specialist training and authority. |

**Use the least invasive method capable of preserving the at-risk information.** Record why ordinary CSU handling wasn't possible and exactly what you did.

## Scene priorities — before the first photograph

- [ ] **Safety and emergency care:** address threats, injuries, fire, traffic, unstable structures and other hazards. Document any necessary lifesaving changes; do not delay emergency care for photography.
- [ ] **Secure a sufficient perimeter:** include approaches, exits and potential evidence outside the obvious center. Restrict unnecessary entry; assign an officer to monitor scene access if possible.
- [ ] **Establish a controlled route:** identify a safe entry/exit path that minimizes new footprints, disturbance, cross-contamination and contact with potential evidence. Do not walk directly toward an item simply for a close-up.
- [ ] **Separate and account for persons:** identify responders, witnesses and other persons entering/leaving. Follow scene-safety, privacy and agency rules.
- [ ] **Notify command / CSU:** record who was called, when, response or unavailability, and any directed interim actions.
- [ ] **Note original conditions:** date/time/timezone, address, weather, lighting, doors/windows, alarms, running equipment, sounds, odors and visible evidence. Clearly distinguish initial observations from what you learn later.
- [ ] **Use approved camera equipment:** ensure battery/storage are sufficient, verify date/time setting and preserve originals. Avoid automatic uploads to personal photo libraries or unapproved cloud accounts.

**Do not move evidence, turn lights on/off, open doors, reposition objects, add markers or place rulers before photographing the scene as found**, unless safety or an authorized emergency requires it. Document and photograph necessary changes afterward.

## The photo sequence: outside → in, overall → midrange → close-up

The goal is to allow someone **who was never at the scene** to understand where the scene was, how its parts connect, exactly where evidence was found, and what the item looked like **before anyone altered it**.

### Pass 1 — Outside and perimeter: establish location

Start from a **safe position outside the scene**. Photograph:

1. Address/building/landmarks, street approach, entrances and exits; include identifying location features when appropriate.
2. The wider property, paths of approach and departure, nearby cameras, windows, gates, vehicles and other potentially relevant areas.
3. **Overlapping overall views** from multiple orientations so the viewer can reconstruct the layout. When practical and safe, cover all sides without crossing the protected scene.

**Why it matters:** A close-up of a phone on a desk cannot tell an examiner **which building, room or desk** it came from.

### Pass 2 — Transition inside: show how areas connect

From the designated safe path:

1. Photograph the threshold/entry point **before crossing or touching anything**, if it can be done safely.
2. Make an overall view looking into each relevant room/area; use multiple corners or doorways where possible **without stepping into potential evidence**.
3. Use overlapping photographs that connect **hallway → doorway → room → workstation/table → evidence location**.
4. Record where photographs were taken and facing direction (notes or simple sketch); avoid guessing measurements.

**Why it matters:** This gives the later examiner spatial context even if the scene is released or changed before CSU arrives.

### Pass 3 — Unmarked evidence views: preserve the original appearance

For each relevant item **before markers or scales are placed**:

- **Overall:** an image of the room/area that includes the item **and surrounding landmarks**.
- **Midrange:** show the item's relationship to furniture, another object, a wall, doorway, cable, charger or connected device.
- **Close-up:** show the item and visible identifiers/condition clearly **without moving it**, using an appropriate angle and focus. Photograph each accessible side/connection *in place*, not by rolling or turning the item.
- For a powered-on phone/computer, photograph only its **already-visible display**, power indicator and cables. Do not unlock, navigate, tap, wake, unplug or reboot for photos; follow the relevant digital-evidence guide.

**Essential rule: capture unaltered views first.** This creates the baseline against which any later marker placement, emergency movement or evidence collection can be explained.

### Pass 4 — Identification placards and scales (only after the unmarked series)

**Evidence markers/placards identify an item. Forensic scales provide size. They serve different purposes.** Do not treat a numbered marker as a ruler.

If authorized, trained, and placement **will not touch/obscure/contaminate evidence**:

1. Assign each visible item a consistent identifier tied to the evidence log and sketch (e.g., `Item 01`, `Item 02`).
2. Place a **clean numbered placard near—not on—the item** where it can be viewed without moving the evidence or stepping through an evidence path.
3. **Repeat wide/overall and midrange images with markers visible and legible.** A wide shot containing Item 01 and Item 02 should show **where each marker sits in the scene**, not merely a collection of close-ups of placards.
4. For a useful **scaled close-up**, first make the same close-up **without** a ruler; then position an appropriate **known-unit forensic scale** adjacent to the feature, preferably **on the same plane** and without touching or covering evidentiary surfaces. Align camera/sensor approximately parallel to the plane being measured to limit distortion.
5. Make the close-up with a readable scale and the relevant detail in focus, plus enough context to associate it with the marked midrange. Recheck focus, framing and glare **without deleting previous frames**.
6. Photograph any necessary placement/removal or scene change; record who placed the marker/scale, where, when, and why.

**Do not force a marker or scale into place.** If placement would disturb blood, prints, fragile trace evidence, a hazard or a live electronic device, **leave it out** and document why. A marker in a distant wide shot is useful for **identification**; accurate measurement generally requires an appropriate scale next to the feature in a **close-up**, not a ruler somewhere across the room.

### What should the resulting photo sequence show?

```text
Photo 001: Outside building from street — address and entry approach
Photo 002: Hallway toward Room 104 — door and orientation
Photo 003: Overall Room 104 from doorway — no markers; desk visible
Photo 004: Midrange desk and laptop/phone — no markers
Photo 005: Close-up phone as found — screen visible, untouched
Photo 006: Overall Room 104 — Item 01 marker near desk, Item 02 near doorway
Photo 007: Midrange desk — Item 01 marker shows phone relationship to laptop
Photo 008: Close-up phone from safe angle — no ruler; condition shown
Photo 009: Detail close-up — clean scale nearby, same plane where feasible
```

*Fictional teaching example. Photo numbers, room descriptions and devices are invented. An actual photo log should capture camera, operator, time, position/direction and changes as required by agency policy.*

## Evidence-specific notes for digital devices

| Item | Photograph while still in place | Avoid changing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Phone/tablet** | Overall location, visible screen/lock status, charger, cable, nearby watch, damage, marker in later context views | Buttons, passcode attempts, notifications, network/power state |
| **Desktop/laptop** | Screen state, power indicator, keyboard, connections, connected drives, wall/network cables, location | Lid, cables, power, mounted encrypted volumes |
| **USB/SD card** | Where found, adapter, visible label, connector, attachment to running computer | Plugging into a computer, ejecting from live host, moving before scene shots |
| **CCTV recorder/camera** | Recorder/camera location, labels, wiring, visible system clock (only if already on display), relevant camera viewpoints | Restarting recorder, changing time/retention, pulling drives |

After documenting, use the dedicated [phone](mobile-device-collection.md), [computer](computer-collection.md), [removable-media](removable-media.md), or [CCTV preservation](video-preservation.md) guide. Scene photography does **not** authorize technical examination or file access.

## Photo log and simple orientation sketch

A camera's timestamp may be inaccurate; filenames alone don't explain location or subject. Record at least the following in the **agency-approved record**, ideally contemporaneously:

```text
Incident / authority:
Reason CSU unavailable or delayed:
Photographer / other personnel:
Date/time (with timezone) and reference clock:
Scene address, entry/exit control, scene conditions:
Approved camera/device, time settings and storage method:
Photo number / original filename:
Position (outside, doorway, corner, hallway) and facing direction:
View type (overall / midrange / close-up / scaled):
Subject and evidence marker IDs:
Marker/scale placement details and any obstruction/limits:
Any scene changes, reason, person authorizing, before/after photos:
Image transfer location, original file handling, custody:
Outstanding CSU requests, urgent risks, unphotographed areas:
```

A **simple not-to-scale sketch** can mark doors, north/reference direction when reliably determined, fixed furniture, evidence numbers, and approximate camera positions. Never invent exact distances; take measured values only with appropriate approved tools and method.

## Keep original photographs intact

- Use an **agency-approved camera or managed device** and evidence-transfer workflow. Consumer personal-photo accounts, automatic personal cloud sync or public messaging are generally inappropriate for case images.
- **Do not delete “bad” frames on camera.** Keep them as part of the original sequence under SOP; take another clear frame and note it.
- Retain original file bytes, filenames, metadata and transfer logs. Don't use beauty filters, AI enhancements, resizing, markup or auto-editing on the **evidentiary original**.
- If a diagram/annotated image is required, produce a **separate derivative** marked as such; retain its source association and note the modifications.
- Follow agency procedures for hashing original files or evidence-export packages, custody and secure storage. Hashes help verify integrity; they do not establish the scene was untouched before your arrival.

## When circumstances force a change

Life safety outranks scene photography. If an object must be moved to save a life, make the area safe, or prevent immediate destruction:

1. Perform the necessary authorized action.
2. Record the **before state** if safely possible; never delay lifesaving care to obtain a photograph.
3. Record who acted, what changed, why, when, and who witnessed it.
4. Photograph the **after state**, collect related notes and tell CSU/examiner during handoff.
5. Never reconstruct or replace an object to make it look as though nothing changed.

## Eight costly mistakes to avoid

1. Walking to the center first and missing scene/context photographs.
2. Placing item numbers or scales **before** showing the scene as found.
3. Using only tight close-ups that don't show where an object was located.
4. Putting a ruler away from the feature or at a different plane and later treating the image as an accurate measurement.
5. Moving phones, turning screens on, separating cables or opening drawers solely for a better image.
6. Failing to document who entered, when, and which scene changes occurred.
7. Deleting/blurring/editing original photographs or sending them through unapproved apps.
8. Treating these preliminary photos as a substitute for a trained CSU response or full examination where one is needed.

## Handoff to CSU or examiner

Tell the receiving specialist:

- **Why you began:** no CSU resources or a specific delay/urgent evidence-loss risk, who authorized interim photography, scene status when you arrived.
- **What you documented:** perimeter, interior paths, overall/mid/close shots, evidence marker map and any scale images.
- **What was not processed:** unopened rooms, potentially fragile/latent/biological evidence, unexamined devices, scenes that were too hazardous to enter.
- **What was changed:** emergency movements, light/door changes, marker placements, device status or unavoidable entry.
- **Where the originals are:** agency evidence-media location, photo log, transfer/custody record and any integrity checks.
