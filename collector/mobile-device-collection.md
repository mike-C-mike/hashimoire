# Smartphone & Tablet — Field Preservation

**Audience:** field collectors | patrol officers | investigators  
**Use case:** collection | preservation | handoff  
**Environment:** iOS | Android | tablets  
**Risk:** caution  
**Guidance reviewed:** 2026-10-21 · Based on SWGDE 18-F-003-2.0 (2025)

> **STOP / CONTACT AN EXAMINER:** An unlocked phone, a device at risk of remote wipe, or a damaged/swollen/wet device requires immediate attention. This page is a field preservation guide, **not** permission to search, extract, unlock, or alter a device. Follow your legal authority, agency SOP, training, and examiner directions.

> **DAMAGE OVERRIDE:** The ordinary advice to preserve a phone's on/unlocked state applies only when it is **safe**. For a smoking, hissing, swollen, leaking, hot or liquid-damaged phone, stop routine handling and use the [damaged-device safety guide](damaged-devices.md). Do not charge a potentially unsafe battery to preserve access. This distinction follows SWGDE 14-F-004-1.1 (2016), SWGDE 18-F-003-2.0 (2025), and current USFA battery-safety guidance.

## First 60 seconds — do this before deciding anything else

- [ ] Confirm that seizure/preservation is **authorized**, and account for any urgent safety concerns.
- [ ] **Photograph before handling:** the device where found, screen as-is, nearby cable/charger, case, paired watch, SIM tray and accessories when visible.
- [ ] Record **date/time and timezone** from an independent source; note visible device clock, notifications and battery status without exploring.
- [ ] Determine whether the phone is **off, on-and-locked, on-and-unlocked, or apparently asleep**. A black screen does not always mean off.
- [ ] Identify hazards: broken battery, heat, moisture, corrosion, biological/trace evidence, or an actively changing screen.
- [ ] **Do not guess a passcode, open an app, browse photos/messages, or press buttons merely to investigate.**
- [ ] Choose the matching condition below and arrange prompt transfer to the examiner.

## Choose the condition you actually see

| Condition | Collector priority | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Powered OFF** (confirmed) | Leave off, document, preserve physical state and transport under approved SOP. | Powering on, connecting a charger “to check,” attempting to unlock. |
| **ON and UNLOCKED** | **Urgent examiner contact.** Preserve current access state with examiner-directed power and isolation planning; document any changes. | Powering off, rebooting, deliberately locking, casual touching or exploration. |
| **ON and LOCKED** | Preserve power and condition; contact examiner for approved isolation and transport. | Guessing codes, forcing a restart, manipulating notifications. |
| **State UNKNOWN / screen dark** | Photograph and document indicators; consult trained personnel before trying to wake it. | Treating a dark screen as confirmation that the phone is off. |
| **DAMAGED / WET / HOT** | **Safety first**; use the [damaged-device guide](damaged-devices.md) and qualified safety/forensic direction. | Applying power, cleaning, charging, drying or routine bagging of unsafe batteries. |

**The practical rule (when safe):** Keep a truly *off* phone off. For a phone that is *on*, avoid loss of power or an unnecessary restart. For an **unlocked** phone, preserve the access opportunity and involve the examiner immediately. In all cases, follow the trained isolation and collection plan rather than improvising.

## When ON and UNLOCKED

**Why this can be urgent:** A device can lock, reboot, or move into a more restrictive security state. Access that was possible on scene may no longer be available at the lab.

1. Photograph the current display **without navigating**.
2. Tell the examiner whether the passcode is **known or unknown** if that information was obtained lawfully. Do not place any password in public Hashimoire.
3. Communicate charging state and any visible warning, incoming remote change, or lockdown condition.
4. Maintain a controlled chain of custody and use only **approved, trained methods** for screen/power preservation and network isolation.
5. Record who authorized *any* state change, what was changed, and the exact time.
6. Prioritize rapid transfer/acquisition coordination. Do **not** install apps, enable developer settings, or change configuration to keep it open without examiner direction.

**Do not overpromise:** Being unlocked does not mean every dataset is accessible or that the examiner can acquire everything.

## When ON but LOCKED

- Document visible lock screen, messages and time, without opening notification contents or guessing passwords.
- Avoid unnecessary reboot/shutdown; discuss a power plan with the examiner.
- Follow approved RF/network preservation procedure, especially when remote alteration or wipe is a concern.
- Do not assume a passcode can be obtained, that biometrics will continue to work, or that the current state can be recreated later.

## When OFF — leave it OFF

- Record your basis for concluding the device is powered down.
- Preserve condition, accessories and original position.
- **Do not plug it in just to see if it works**; some phones boot automatically when connected to power.
- If damage, heat, contamination or water exposure is involved, coordinate with trained personnel before packaging or changing anything.

## Network isolation: no magic switch

Devices can receive commands, sync content and potentially be remotely erased while connected. Isolation helps protect evidence, **but altering settings or radio state is itself an evidentiary intervention**.

- **Airplane mode does not guarantee all radios are off.** Wi-Fi, Bluetooth/BLE and other connections can remain active or reconnect depending on OS/settings.
- **Faraday bags are not perfect.** Shielding can fail; the device may drain its battery more quickly while searching for service.
- **Power and RF shielding must be planned together.** Do not run an unshielded charging cable out of a Faraday enclosure in a way that compromises isolation.
- Only use tested RF equipment and procedures you are trained and authorized to perform. If suitable equipment is unavailable, notify the examiner rather than experimenting.

**Document** what isolation method was used, when, whether device power was supported, and any observed limitations.

## Physical handling and collection

- Photograph any visible identifiers **without exploring the device**; include case, damage, cables, attachments and where it was found.
- Consider latent prints, DNA and trace evidence. Coordinate examination order with other forensic disciplines *before* wiping, peeling, cleaning, or packaging.
- Preserve associations between phone, watch, charging cable, removable cards, and other relevant accessories, but label separate items clearly.
- Do not remove SIM cards, manipulate eSIM settings, pair/unpair a watch, or plug into an unknown data source unless instructed.
- If hazardous or wet: prioritize personnel/battery safety and trained specialist procedures rather than generic bagging instructions.
- Never pack an actively overheating/swollen battery in a way that traps heat or creates an unsafe transport condition.

## What the receiving examiner needs to know

Use the [evidence handoff checklist](evidence-handoff.md). Communicate:

| Record | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Initial state | On/off, locked/unlocked, screen dark, device charging, battery indicator |
| First observation | Date/time/timezone, visible notifications, scene location, other connected devices |
| Device identity | Visible make/model/serial/IMEI when available **without unlocking** |
| Changes made | Button presses, connections, isolation settings, charging, packaging, restarts, by whom and when |
| Risk and urgency | Unlocked screen, unknown passcode, network risk, temperature or water damage |
| Custody | Seizing officer, each transfer, seal/media labels, location and condition |

**Example handoff description (fictional):** “Smartphone recovered on a desk at 14:26 CDT. Screen illuminated and unlocked with an open home screen. Photographed without navigating. No buttons pressed. Examiner notified at 14:28. Approved isolation method applied at 14:31 by trained personnel, recorded separately. Device transferred in powered-on condition at 14:48.”

## Five mistakes that are expensive to fix

1. Powering off an unlocked phone before an examiner can assess it.
2. Turning on a phone that was off merely to identify it.
3. Assuming airplane mode disables every radio, or assuming an untested Faraday bag always works.
4. Repeated passcode attempts or casual tapping around the screen.
5. Delivering a phone to the lab without initial power/lock state, intervention notes, or timely notice of an unlocked device.

## When this guide is not enough

Stop and escalate for an **unlocked screen**, suspected remote wipe, a time-sensitive preservation issue, damaged/hazardous battery, water contamination, MDM/enterprise controls, paired wearable dependency, unusual radio/isolation behavior, or an operationally critical device.
