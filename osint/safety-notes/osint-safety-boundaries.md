# Safety Notes

This folder contains OSINT safety boundaries, ethical rules, and documentation guidance.

OSINT can reduce risk when it is scoped and defensive. It can create harm when it becomes invasive, excessive, personal, or disconnected from a legitimate purpose.

## Purpose

Safety notes help define:

- what belongs in Hashimoire
- what should be excluded
- how to minimize harm
- how to document public information responsibly
- when to stop
- when to escalate
- how to separate facts from assumptions

## Folder Contents

- `osint-safety-boundaries.md`  
  A detailed safety guide for defensive OSINT work.

## Core Rule

Defensive OSINT should reduce risk, not create it.

What question am I trying to answer?
What information is necessary to answer it?
What information should I avoid collecting?
What information should I avoid storing?
What information should I avoid repeating in the report?

For most OSINT reviews, do not engage with accounts, subjects, scammers, impersonators, or third parties.

Do not:

message suspicious accounts
warn possible scammers directly
attempt to bait a response
create fake accounts
join private groups
request access
contact friends or family
contact employers
contact schools
pressure platforms from personal accounts

Engagement can escalate risk, contaminate documentation, alert a target, or create safety problems.

If engagement is necessary, it should be authorized, documented, and handled through the proper channel.
